# Brief: Albania Travel

## The job

Rebuild albania-travel.com.au with new design and new content. Same business,
same honesty, presented so the destination and the two people behind the
service carry the site instead of a stock WordPress theme.

## The problem with the current site

The business proposition is genuinely good and the content behind it is real:
three well-shaped private tours, a direct relationship with the local
operator, five detailed real testimonials, and an unusually rich library of
destination writing (thirty-plus places described with specifics, a proper
FAQ). But the presentation undercuts it.

- **The brand is honesty, and the imagery includes AI-generated pictures.**
  The founding pitch is "fewer middlemen, no inflated margins", and the
  homepage illustrates it with files named `Gemini_Generated_Image_*.png` and
  Unsplash stock. A visitor cannot tell the genuine Albania photography from
  the synthetic filler, which quietly taxes the credibility of everything
  else.
- **The two people are the differentiator and the site hides them.** Geoff and
  James publish their personal mobiles and do the planning themselves. That
  is the actual product: an Australian you can ring. The current site
  presents them as a small "ambassadors" footnote.
- **The destination writing is the strongest content and it is one long
  page.** Thirty-plus places, all described with real specifics, buried in a
  single scroll under a generic template. The material deserves structure: it
  is what convinces someone Albania is worth nine days.
- **The tours read as products, not as shapes.** All three are private and
  fully customisable, which is the point, but the layout presents them like
  fixed packages in a catalogue.

## What this build does about it

1. **Leads with the proposition, in their own words.** Fewer middlemen,
   sensible prices, a private car and driver, and an Australian on the phone.
   The founding sentence is the best copy on the site; use it.
2. **Puts Geoff and James where the product is.** Named, pictured, phone
   numbers visible. The enquiry path is "talk to a person", and the design
   treats that as the primary call to action on every page.
3. **Gives the destination writing real structure.** The places become a
   browsable section organised by the way travellers actually think (coast,
   mountains, living history), feeding the tours rather than sitting beside
   them.
4. **Presents the tours as private shapes to start from.** Duration, price
   basis, what is always included, and what "customisable" concretely means,
   with the day-rhythm rather than a rigid day-by-day table.
5. **Uses only genuine photography.** The client's real destination
   photography is harvested first (Hard Rule 14). AI-generated files from
   their library are recorded but not reused. Where genuine coverage runs
   out, curated stock of the actual places is the fallback, credited
   honestly, which their own site already does implicitly.

## What stays honest

- **Only the client's published numbers are used**: the three tour prices,
  the durations, the inclusions, and the destination facts from their own
  pages. No invented traveller counts, no "98% satisfaction", no years-in-
  business claim because they do not publish one (Hard Rule 4).
- **Testimonials are the five real published ones**, verbatim, with the
  attribution level the client uses. None invented.
- **No invented bios.** Geoff and James have first names, photos and roles.
  That is what is published and that is what ships.
- **The Wander Albania relationship is stated**, because the client states it
  and because it is the mechanism behind the price honesty.

## Deliberate omissions

- **No booking engine, no cart, no live pricing.** The business is
  conversational; the site's job is to start the conversation.
- **No blog.** Not requested, and the destination library does that job
  better.
- **No generic travel-agency tropes**: no passport-and-map flatlays, no
  countdown timers, no "limited seats" urgency. Their gentle "the secret is
  getting out" is the only urgency the brand needs.

## Success looks like

An experienced Australian traveller lands on the home page and, within one
screen, understands that Albania is worth nine days, that these tours are
private and sensibly priced, and that the next step is a phone call with a
named person, not a checkout.
